# Source selection: 2025 SHED financial well-being

Decision date: 2026-08-02

## Concept and estimand

Select the Federal Reserve's 2025 Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking (SHED) for self-reported current financial well-being among U.S. adults. The initial source benchmark is the weighted share reporting that they are doing okay or living comfortably (`atleast_okay="Yes"`). This is a subjective status measure; it is not a balance-sheet, income, poverty, or time-use measure.

## Local catalog comparison at selection time

| Candidate | Local status | Unit and universe | Relevant measure | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SHED 2025 | Not yet promoted when selected | Adult respondent; cross-sectional U.S. adult population represented by the survey | `atleast_okay`; emergency-expense and financial-resilience items | Selected: direct concept match |
| SCF 2022 | Validated local release | Primary economic unit | Wealth, debt, income, financial-emergency items | Not comparable: excellent balance-sheet measure, but different unit, period, and concept |
| ACS PUMS 2024 | Validated local release | Person | Income and poverty ratio | Not comparable: objective income/poverty, not subjective financial well-being |
| CPS ASEC 2025 | Validated local release | Person in official poverty universe | Income and official poverty category | Not comparable: official poverty estimand, not perceived financial well-being |
| ATUS 2024 | Validated local release | Person-day respondent | Daily time use | Not comparable: no equivalent financial-well-being construct |

The exact phrase “financial well being” was not found in the local catalog. Broader searches found SCF financial-characteristic documentation, SCF/CPS income variables, ACS/CPS poverty variables, and SCF wealth documentation. Those are adjacent concepts, not substitutes.

## Design and files

- **Release year:** 2025.
- **Official source:** Federal Reserve SHED data index and 2025 codebook/report.
- **Expected records:** 12,934 respondents, as stated in the official codebook.
- **Cross-sectional published-statistics weight:** `weight`.
- **Population-scaled weight:** `weight_pop`.
- **Panel weights:** `panel_weight` and `panel_weight_pop`; these are not interchangeable with the cross-sectional weights.
- **Variance design:** the public release supplies analysis weights but no replicate-weight set. Point estimates can reproduce official weighted results; publication uncertainty requires a documented approximation or an official variance source and must not pretend a full design is available.
- **Imputation:** variable-level `*_iflag` fields identify imputation where supplied. Missing, imputed, and out-of-universe values must remain distinguishable.
- **Artifacts:** official CSV ZIP, Stata ZIP, PDF codebook, and annual report. Same-URL upstream changes are retained as new immutable release revisions.

## Benchmark and revision gates

- Reproduce the report’s rounded 73 percent “doing okay or living comfortably” result using `atleast_okay="Yes"` weighted by `weight`; absolute tolerance is 0.005 because the official result is published to a whole percentage point.
- Require 12,934 rows, unique `shedid`, release year 2025, positive cross-sectional and population weights, and matching Stata row metadata.
- Require `BK58` to be absent. The Federal Reserve states that the 2025 data were revised on July 7, 2026 to remove that variable.

## Limitations

SHED is an online survey fielded through Ipsos KnowledgePanel. Nonresponse, panel recruitment, question wording, mode, and annual questionnaire changes can affect comparability. The 2025 public file includes panel fields, but longitudinal analysis must use the panel universe and panel weights rather than silently treating it as repeated cross-sections. Descriptive associations are not causal effects.
