# EIA Form 861 (2024) — source selection note

## Decision

Use the U.S. Energy Information Administration's final 2024 Form 861 files for utility-level residential revenue, sales, customer counts, ownership, and balancing-authority assignments.

## Alternatives considered

- **Electric Power Monthly:** provides authoritative state and national price benchmarks, but not utility ownership or TVA distributor identifiers.
- **Retail electricity provider websites:** not standardized, not complete, and not suitable for a national ownership comparison.
- **Form 861:** selected because the annual utility census contains the required revenue, sales, ownership, service-type, and balancing-authority fields in one official release.

## Record unit and universe

The normalized record is one utility × state × service-type row. The retained analysis uses rows marked observed (`O`) and all service types so bundled, energy-only, and delivery-only charges remain represented in restructured markets.

## Variables and definitions

- Residential revenue: EIA's residential-sector revenue, published in thousand dollars.
- Residential sales: megawatthours sold to residential ultimate customers.
- Average residential price: `sum(revenue) / sum(sales) × 100`, cents per kilowatt-hour.
- Ownership: EIA's utility ownership classification; municipal utilities are treated as publicly owned.
- TVA area: utilities with balancing-authority code `TVA`. This is the retail footprint of TVA distributors, not TVA's wholesale rate.

## Design and uncertainty

Form 861 is an administrative census of electric utilities rather than a sample survey. Sampling standard errors do not apply. Ownership comparisons remain observational because service areas differ in geography, customer density, regulation, and cost structure.

## Validation

The adapter requires the official final-data ZIP, the `Sales_Ult_Cust_2024.xlsx` workbook, the published `States` schema, observed records, the four major ownership classes, and plausible positive residential revenue and sales. The retained analysis benchmarks its computed U.S. average of 15.41 cents per kilowatt-hour against EIA Electric Power Monthly's 2024 annual residential average of 16.48 cents per kilowatt-hour; the 0.935 ratio passes the declared ±10% gate.

## Provenance and reuse

The retained analysis records immutable release `eia_861-2024-aca25b50f1cb` and the official ZIP checksum in `diagnostics.json`. EIA states that its data and files may be used and distributed with acknowledgment unless a specific item says otherwise. Raw provider files stay in the local release store rather than Git.
