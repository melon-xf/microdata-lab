# Financial Well-Being by Race/Ethnicity, 2025

## Methods

Analysis of the 2025 Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking (SHED). The weighted share of respondents reporting they are "at least okay financially" was computed for each race/ethnicity category using SHED analysis weights. Standard errors are simple proportion SEs; 95% confidence intervals use the normal approximation.

## Results

| Race/ethnicity | % At least okay | 95% CI | Sample n |
|---|---|---|---|
| White | 78.6% | [77.7%, 79.5%] | 8,650 |
| Hispanic | 61.8% | [59.5%, 64.1%] | 1,758 |
| Black | 60.2% | [57.7%, 62.7%] | 1,509 |
| Asian | 82.4% | [79.5%, 85.3%] | 658 |
| Other | 59.2% | [54.2%, 64.2%] | 359 |

Asian (82.4%) and White (78.6%) households report the highest financial well-being. Black (60.2%), Hispanic (61.8%), and Other (59.2%) households report substantially lower rates. The 18–22 percentage point gap persists after weighting.

## Limitations

- Proportion SEs are design-naive (no replicate weights in public release).
- `race_5cat` collapses diverse groups; "Other" is heterogeneous.
- Financial well-being is self-reported and subjective.
- Association is descriptive, not causal.
