# Question — REA rural electrification, 1930–1963

## Estimand
The share of U.S. farms receiving central-station electric service, by year,
1930–1963 — the empirical record of the New Deal's rural electrification
program and the pace at which a public agency with public financing
electrified the countryside.

## Why
The chart measures the pace of rural electrification before and after the
federal REA program. About 9% of farms had electricity in 1930, 30.4% did by
April 1940, and 96.5% did by 1960.

## Universe
All U.S. farms, as counted by the decennial Census of Agriculture
(denominator), with REA annual estimates of farms served by central-station
electricity (numerator). Published national aggregates; no microdata.

## Variables
- `year`, `percent` (share of farms with electricity), `farms` (number of
  farms receiving central-station service).

## Design
- Every point is read directly from a scanned USDA Agricultural Statistics
  yearbook edition (archive.org full-text + word-coordinate verification);
  the exact table is cited per row in the README.
- REA charter marked at 1935 (Executive Order 7037, May 11, 1935; Rural
  Electrification Act of May 20, 1936).

## Assumptions / limitations
- "Farms receiving central-station electric service" (REA definition) is
  used consistently. The Census-based 1940 figure (33.2% of farm dwellings
  with electricity) is slightly higher; noted, not used.
- Published aggregates; no sampling variance is published.
- A TVA residential-rate comparison series was considered but could not be
  verified from the cited TVA publications, so it is omitted.

## Sources
USDA, *Agricultural Statistics* 1940 (Table 745), 1950 (Table 742),
1955 (Table 777), 1961 (Table 807), 1964 (Table 813); 1930 point from the
Census of Agriculture as tabulated in the 1940 edition.
